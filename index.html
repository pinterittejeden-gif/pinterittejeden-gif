These are desk studies, not lab results.

I haven't built or validated these systems. The references are here so you can see what comes from published work and what is still only my idea.

open notebooks / 04

Four ideas I keep coming back to.

Some are more plausible than others. Each notebook has sources, sketches, and the point where the idea starts to wobble.

01 ✦ root signal / optical readout

plant reporters + soil

BioLume Sentinel

Could a plant point out the patches of soil worth testing? I started with reporter systems, then followed the idea into the much messier reality of mixed soil.

Where it gets difficult The signal has to be specific enough to mean something.

02 ⌁ texture / settlement / retrieval

reef restoration + settlement

Coral-Suture

Instead of another permanent reef object, could a small mineral tile be designed for larvae and later retrieved? The useful part may be the tile, not the grander system around it.

Where it gets difficult Settlement changes with species, site, and season.

03 ◫ uptake / transport / capture

phytoremediation + wood

Metallophyte-Armor

I wanted to know whether a tree could move copper into the tissue where it might do useful preservation work. The notebook became a mass-balance problem very quickly.

Where it gets difficult Uptake is not the same as delivery to the right tissue.

Open the notebook ↗
04 ⌘ damage / local growth / stop

fungal growth + materials

Titan-Mycelium

This began as a self-healing material idea. It became more interesting when I limited the fungus to one sealed, replaceable coupon and one finite pulse of growth.

Where it gets difficult The growth has to start on cue — and stop just as reliably.

how I use the notebook

Part sketchbook, part reality check.

I keep the references close to the drawings. That makes it harder to accidentally present a nice-looking connection as an established fact.

  1. 01

    Start with the paper trail.

    If the mechanism comes from published work, I link it.

  2. 02

    Mark the leap.

    The point where I connect two ideas is usually the interesting bit — and the least certain.

  3. 03

    Leave a way out.

    Every project needs a result that would make me drop it or redraw it.
