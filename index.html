before you read

These are desk studies, not lab results.

No pipettes were harmed in the making of this website. I haven't built or validated these systems. I link the references so you can see where the science ends and my questionable enthusiasm begins.

open notebooks / 05

Five ideas currently refusing to leave.

Some are sensible. Some probably arrived too late at night. All of them have sources, sketches, and at least one part that makes the whole thing wobble.

01 ✦ root signal / optical readout

plant reporters + soil

BioLume Sentinel

Could a plant point out the patches of soil worth testing? It sounds tidy until you remember that soil is biological soup with rocks in it. The notebook starts with reporter systems and ends in the mess.

Where it gets difficult The signal has to be specific enough to mean something.

02 ⌁ texture / settlement / retrieval

reef restoration + settlement

Coral-Suture

Could a small mineral tile give coral larvae somewhere useful to settle, then be retrieved later? The tile may be useful. The grand system around it may just enjoy having a dramatic name.

Where it gets difficult Settlement changes with species, site, and season.

03 ◫ uptake / transport / capture

phytoremediation + wood

Metallophyte-Armor

Could a tree move copper into the tissue where it might help preserve wood? The notebook became a mass-balance problem almost immediately. Trees, unhelpfully, do not read engineering requirements.

Where it gets difficult Uptake is not the same as delivery to the right tissue.

04 ⌘ damage / local growth / stop

fungal growth + materials

Titan-Mycelium

Self-healing materials sound excellent until you ask the material to stop healing. I limited the fungus to one sealed, replaceable coupon and one finite pulse of growth. The fungus did not sign off on this plan.

Where it gets difficult The growth has to start on cue - and stop just as reliably.

05 ⇢ donor / parasite bridge / receiver

plant trafficking + parasitism

Ghost-Graft

Could dodder be bullied into doing delivery work? Let it bridge two plants, move one traceable protein across, then cut it off before it remembers that it is a crop pest. Natural traffic is real. Controlled cargo is very much not.

Where it gets difficult The courier drains its clients and may deliver viruses for free.

how I use the notebook

Draw the exciting bit. Then try to ruin it.

I keep the references close to the drawings. This makes it harder to promote a nice-looking connection to established fact by accident. It also ruins several excellent diagrams.

  1. 01

    Start with the paper trail.

    If the mechanism comes from published work, I link it. “Trust me” is not a citation.

  2. 02

    Mark the leap.

    The point where I connect two ideas is usually the interesting bit and the least certain. Very convenient.

  3. 03

    Leave a way out.

    Every project needs a result that would make me drop it or redraw it. Otherwise it is a belief with nice typography.
